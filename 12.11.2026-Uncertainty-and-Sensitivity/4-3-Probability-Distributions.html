Probability distributions for uncertain LCA data

An inventory value is rarely known exactly. Instead of one number we describe it with a probability distribution. LCA software such as openLCA offers four types: lognormal, normal, triangular and uniform. Pick a type, try its LCA examples, move the parameters, and draw random samples as a Monte Carlo simulation would. Example values are illustrative.

“Exact” comes from the formula; “samples” from the random draws – the more samples, the closer they get.

When to use which?

TypeShapeUse it when …LCA examplesWatch out
LognormalSkewed to the right, never negativeValues are positive and the uncertainty is a factor (“within a factor of 3”). Default in ecoinvent (pedigree matrix).Emission factors, field emissions (N₂O), toxic emissions, most inventory flowsMean > median; a large GSD² gives a long tail of rare high values.
NormalSymmetric bellMeasured values with random, symmetric error around a known mean.Product mass, meter readings, lab analyses, yieldsCan produce negative values when the SD is large compared with the mean.
TriangularPeak at the most likely value, hard limitsAn expert gives a minimum, a most likely value and a maximum.Transport distance, lifetime, loading of a machineLimits are absolute – nothing outside [min, max].
UniformFlat between two limitsOnly a range is known, no value is more likely than another.Recycled content, supplier shares, loss ratesGives the extremes as much weight as the middle – often too pessimistic.
