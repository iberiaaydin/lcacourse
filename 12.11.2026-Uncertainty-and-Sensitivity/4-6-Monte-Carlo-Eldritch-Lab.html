Eldritch Lab

A Monte Carlo Experiment

A spellcaster fires beams of energy at a target. Every beam needs a dice roll to hit, and another dice roll for damage. How much damage does one spell deal? There is no single answer – there is a probability distribution. This lab estimates it by Monte Carlo simulation (casting the spell thousands of times with random dice) and checks the estimate against the exact distribution worked out with probability theory.

No game knowledge needed: hover the i icons, or see the glossary. Dice notation: 1d20, 1d10, 2d10.

    Understanding Monte Carlo Simulation

    Monte Carlo simulation estimates probabilities and expected outcomes by generating random events over and over again and counting what happens. Here, the computer casts Eldritch Blast thousands of times – rolling every die with a random number generator – to estimate the Warlock's damage distribution. Because this game is simple, we can also work out the exact answer and see how close the simulation gets.

    How accurate is the estimate?

    Run a simulation to see the confidence interval and the error.
    95 % CI ≈ x ± 1.96 · s / √N    absolute error = |x − E[D]|     relative error = |x − E[D]| / E[D]

    This is an approximate confidence interval: it relies on the central limit theorem, so it is rough for small samples (N = 10 or 100). About 95 % of runs produce an interval that contains the exact value – so roughly 1 run in 20 will miss it, and that is expected.

    Why does accuracy improve with N? The standard error s/√N shrinks with the square root of N: 100× more simulations give a 10× narrower interval. Any single run can still land high or low – the fluctuations get smaller, they do not disappear.
