Statistical terms, visually

The next two pages – probability distributions (4-3) and Monte Carlo simulation (4-4) – describe uncertain values with a few statistical terms. Each section below lets you play with one group of terms.

1 · Mean, median, mode 2 · Standard deviation & CV 3 · Percentiles & 95 % interval 4 · Geometric mean & GSD² 5 · Rank correlation ρ

1 · Mean, median and mode – three kinds of “typical value”

Commute times of a group of students, in minutes. Drag the dots left and right (dots with the same value stack up), or add an extreme value.

Values sorted from smallest to largest (the median is highlighted)

Mean (arithmetic average)
Sum of all values ÷ number of values. It is the balance point – the ▲ under the axis where the dots would balance like a seesaw.
Median
The middle value: half of the values are below it, half above. With an even count it is the average of the two middle values.
Mode (most likely value)
The value that occurs most often – the highest stack, or the peak of a distribution curve.

Used in 4-3: the vertical lines on every distribution · in 4-4: “Monte Carlo mean” vs “best guess” (the best guess of a triangle is its mode, of a lognormal its median).

2 · Standard deviation (SD) and coefficient of variation (CV) – how spread out?

The same commute times (change them in section 1). Each line shows how far a value lies from the mean – its deviation. The SD is a typical size of these deviations.

Standard deviation
Square every deviation, average them (divide by n − 1), take the square root. Large deviations count extra because they are squared.
Coefficient of variation (CV)
SD ÷ mean, in %. Compares spread across values of different size: ±5 g on a 310 g mug is small, ±5 g on a 9 g paper cup is large.

The 68 – 95 rule for a normal (bell-shaped) distribution

Used in 4-3: the normal distribution's σ · in 4-4: “Standard deviation” and “Coefficient of variation” in the result statistics.

4 · Geometric mean and GSD² – spread as a factor

For lognormal values (most LCA inventory data in ecoinvent) the spread is not “± so many units” but “× or ÷ a factor”. The geometric mean equals the median. GSD² is the factor: 95 % of the values lie between median ÷ GSD² and median × GSD².

5 · Rank correlation ρ – does a high input go with a high result?

Each dot is one Monte Carlo iteration: the value drawn for an input (x) and the model result (y). The Spearman rank correlation ρ runs from −1 to +1. 4-4 uses ρ² to estimate how much of the result's uncertainty comes from each input.

Used in 4-4: “Which input drives the uncertainty?” – contribution ≈ ρ² of each input, scaled to 100 %.
