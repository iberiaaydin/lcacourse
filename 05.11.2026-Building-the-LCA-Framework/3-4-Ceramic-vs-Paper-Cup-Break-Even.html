Ceramic cup vs paper cup: when does reuse pay off?

Set up a usage scenario: how long one reusable cup is used, how many coffees (300 mL) a day, and how often it is washed. A paper cup is thrown away after every coffee. The page shows the climate change result (kg CO₂-eq, ILCD midpoint) and where each reusable option breaks even with the paper cup. Data: GreenDelta case study “Ceramic cup vs. Paper cup” (Martin, Bunsen & Ciroth, 2018; openLCA 1.7.2, ecoinvent 3.4), Table 2 and Fig. 14.

What the study concludes

11 uses

Ceramic mug in a modern dishwasher breaks even with the paper cup on climate change.

13 uses

Ceramic cup with lid in the dishwasher – the lid adds production and washing.

89 uses / never

Washed by hand with hot water: the mug needs 89 uses, the cup with lid never breaks even.

cold water

Hand-washing the mug with cold water was the best option in 13 of 15 impact categories – heating the water is what counts.

Model: impact = cups × P + washes × W, with P = one cup's production, transport, breakage and disposal and W = one washing (both kg CO₂-eq). P and W were fitted to the study's Table 2 (750 uses) and Fig. 14 (1 use); the fit gives back the study's break-even points. The scenario assumes one cup lasts the whole period; washes = uses ÷ washing interval. The break-even point is P ÷ (paper cup − W ÷ washing interval) uses, the same in both chart views. Cold water is from Table 8 (mug only); washing less often extends the idea of Fig. 11. Values are for teaching.
