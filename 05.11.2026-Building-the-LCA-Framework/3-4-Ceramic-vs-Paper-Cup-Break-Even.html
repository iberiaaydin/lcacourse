Ceramic cup vs paper cup: when does reuse pay off?

Functional unit: 750 coffees of 300 mL served in Berlin. A paper cup is thrown away after every drink; a reusable cup is used N times before it is replaced, so 750 / N cups are needed. Move the slider to see how the climate change result (kg CO₂-eq, ILCD midpoint) changes and where each reusable option breaks even with the paper cup. Data: GreenDelta case study “Ceramic cup vs. Paper cup” (Martin, Bunsen & Ciroth, 2018; openLCA 1.7.2, ecoinvent 3.4), Table 2 and Fig. 14.

What the study concludes

11 uses

Ceramic mug in a modern dishwasher breaks even with the paper cup on climate change.

13 uses

Ceramic cup with lid in the dishwasher – the lid adds production and washing.

89 uses / never

Washed by hand with hot water: the mug needs 89 uses, the cup with lid never breaks even.

cold water

Hand-washing the mug with cold water was the best option in 13 of 15 impact categories – heating the water is what counts.

Model behind the curves: impact = (750 / N) × P + (750 / washing interval) × W, where P is one cup's production, transport, breakage and disposal and W is one washing. P and W were fitted to the study's Table 2 (N = 750) and Fig. 14 (N = 1); the fit gives back the study's break-even points. The cold-water option is from Table 8 and was reported for the mug only. Washing every 2nd or 3rd use follows the idea of Fig. 11. Values are for teaching; see the study for the full 15 impact categories.
